# Query 10 / st_01: Directions derived from observed errors

Observed errors often preserved a familiar scientific explanation while attaching it to the wrong diagram quantity, region, or state. The following opportunities are grounded in the reviewed examples; select those supported by the supplied material.

- **Measured spans followed by a mechanism.** Use the actual endpoints of arrows or brackets to distinguish quantities such as amplitude, peak-to-peak displacement, half-wavelength, and wavelength. Then make that distinction consequential for a further inference. In a reviewed interference item, the model selected the correct phase relationship but doubled a span that already represented twice the incoming amplitude. A useful alternative can preserve the interference law while misreading one measured span.
- **Physical roles bound to depicted regions.** Make a familiar mechanism depend on tracing a leader line to its endpoint, identifying both sides of an interface, or combining the depicted axis with incoming-light direction. In one core item, the selected explanation had the right compositional effects at the wrong interface. A harder revision can retain those scientifically plausible effects while requiring the correct regional assignment. Similar diagrams were also answered correctly, so a topic or the presence of labels alone does not establish difficulty.
- **Recorded state combined with active constraints.** When a diagram records an earlier motion, use its geometry together with the stated active conditions to explain the relevant mechanism. The reviewed fault item required reconciling an old displacement with compression and the separation of matching contacts. Copying a prominent historical arrow gave the wrong account. Preserve this joint dependency when the material supports it.

For relative-history questions, distinguish relations established by actual contacts from relations left unconstrained. An intrusion cutting some layers can establish those age relations without fixing its age relative to a layer it never reaches. This is a secondary direction supported by one reviewed error.

Keep the decisive diagram-to-mechanism links in the authoring analysis. In the question, give clear references without translating those links into their scientific meaning. Revisions should preserve the need to resolve them; explicitly naming every region, converting every span, or replacing the task with generic mechanism recall can remove the observed challenge.
