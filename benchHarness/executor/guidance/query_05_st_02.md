# Query 05 / st_02: Directions derived from observed errors

The main observed failure was a definite negative conclusion when the requested space was not fully observed. Errors clustered in stocked shelves and in street scenes with obstructed local regions. Treat the following as opportunities when supported by the supplied images.

- **Visible stock versus full storage depth.** Look for a well-defined lane or compartment whose visible products make a particular color, product type, or package seem absent, while a specific rear space remains unobserved and could hold that target. A closed container with plainly inaccessible contents was already answered correctly; the more promising cases require separating a readable display from its incomplete coverage. Confirm that the unseen space could actually change the conclusion.
- **Scene coverage versus target coverage.** A wide street view or several camera images can still leave the relevant strip behind a vehicle or the interior of a cab unresolved. Use the supplied views to establish what they jointly reveal about that exact region. Extra images are useful only when their relationship to the target matters.
- **Visible count versus total count.** A tray with one visible bag may still have an unresolved total. This is a secondary opportunity when a genuine hidden region could change whether the threshold is met. Raising the threshold above everything visibly present, without inspecting the available space, is insufficient.

When revising an existing difficult question, retain the evidence dependency that made it difficult. For example, changing “a matching carton behind this box” into “at least two matching cartons in a wider region” removes that dependency if two cartons are plainly visible beside the box. A changed answer class is acceptable when justified; changing the class alone is not evidence of a harder question. Prefer revisions that sharpen the target and scope while preserving the need to assess coverage. Keep the coverage analysis in the authoring process rather than announcing the decisive gap in the question.
